# Patchback: a patch you can open, recovered from a sound

Given a recording of an electronic instrument, Patchback returns an editable, executable DSP patch
and the note events that play it, renders them, and reports how close the rendering is to the
recording. The output is a program a musician can open and change, never PCM from a neural decoder
(DESIGN.md § 1, ADR-005).

## Operator intent

"Patchback is the name of the thing we want to build. Also, it's the name of the repo you shall
create under the beyond10x org. New public project. ... We first create all the specification."
(operator, 2026-10-22, relayed by b10x-chef-orchestrator)

## Who it is for

- Electronic musicians who hear a synth sound they want and have only the recording.
- Sound designers who want a starting patch to edit, not a sample to loop.
- Engineers who want a reproducible, inspectable inverse-synthesis pipeline in one Rust codebase.

## The problem

- A recording is opaque audio. The recipe behind it (oscillator, drive, filter, envelope, gain) is
  gone, and so is the score that played it.
- Several programs can sound nearly the same (DESIGN.md § 9.3), so the goal is an audible match that
  is valid and simple, not the producer's exact settings.
- A neural decoder that emits audio gives nothing to edit and nothing to check (DESIGN.md § 1).

## What success looks like, per milestone (DESIGN.md § 13)

| Milestone | Success |
| --- | --- |
| A | A reference generated by our own synth yields a comparable patch with no neural network, and a deterministic audio-loss improvement over the default patch |
| B | A ModelSpec-trained regressor measurably reduces matching work or improves audio fit |
| C | An isolated bass or lead phrase in, an audible reconstruction and an editable program out, with timing and pitch uncertainty stated |
| D | The system selects and drives meaningful module combinations, not only eight knobs |
| E | Structured electronic music is reconstructed from stems; full-mix separation stays a separate research problem |

The first quality review sets a go/no-go target of at least 20% median normalized loss reduction
against the default patch (DESIGN.md § 12.2). It is an experiment target, not a promise.

## Principles

- Editable program over opaque audio: the output is a patch plus events, rendered by the same engine
  that made the training data (DESIGN.md § 2).
- Determinism: fixed seed, build, input and configuration give reproducible offline results
  (§ 2, invariant 5).
- No third-party ML, DSP, maths or serialization crates; the engine, analysis, search and inference
  adapter are internal Rust (§ 3).
- ModelSpec trains the parameter regressor, pinned to a tested release; no capability is assumed
  that its documentation does not list as shipped (§ 1.1, ADR-001).
- ESS specifies the contracts and is never a runtime or ML dependency (ADR-008).
- Refuse rather than guess: non-finite input, unreliable features and ambiguous pitch produce
  diagnostics (§ 6.2, § 6.3, § 12.3).

## What it is not (DESIGN.md § 2 non-goals, MVP)

- Source separation from a mastered stereo mix.
- Polyphonic or multi-instrument transcription, drum recognition, genre-level arrangement.
- Arbitrary user-defined DSP code or arbitrary feedback loops.
- Real-time training, low-latency inference or DAW-plugin integration.
- Identification of the exact historical patch the producer used.
- A claim that ModelSpec supports arbitrary audio models, convolution, attention, GPU training or
  waveform backpropagation today.
